# Documentation Writing

Your target audience are English readers of varying proficiency. Assume that the target audience are not native English readers or speakers. Always apply the `unslop` skill.

Apply these additional rules to all writing as well:
- Avoid the use of marketing and/or ambiguous language.
- Avoid "it's not x, but y" language.
- Never use passive language when you can use active.
- If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.

## Type of Document
Pick one type of document that you're writing and follow the below guidelines for each.

**How-To Articles** - A strong how-to article focuses on one specific task and gives clear step-by-step instructions. Use language readers can easily understand in all scenarios. Use short, clear, well-defined steps that are easy to follow in a logical order. Explain steps that are not simple or require additional understanding. Explain the flags used for commands with 1-2 simple sentences for a given step.

**Spec Sheet** - Only use these rules when a user has specifically asked for a spec sheet or specification document.Identify the product and its purpose, then clearly organize its features, dimensions, materials, performance requirements, standards, and other essential details. Use precise, testable language using the ADS STE-100 Simplified Technical English standard. `/grill` the user over things that are not clear or easy to discern from provided soure materials. Verify the accuracy and consistency of the contents of the sheet.

**Technical Reference** - Follow the writing rules for ADS-STE100 Simplified Technical English and the `unslop` skill: active voice, short sentences, sentence-case headings, no em dashes, no marketing words. Put identifiers in backticks. A technical reference should have accurate, complete, and verified (and verifiable) information. Good documentation is easy to navigate and search, your documentation should lead with a clear table of contents.

**Tutorial Writing** - State what the reader will build first. Break the goal into small, digestible steps that are easy to follow. Then describe each step the reader will follow. For each step, describe what the reader should see for its output. The end goal is to teach for a reader who is learning by doing themselves.
